# 05. Synthetic data and model

Everything here runs as Python scripts in `ml/`. It produces the history the app displays, the scores the app looks up, and the evidence that the model beats the simple rule. Nothing in `ml/` is deployed.

Synthetic data means invented customers generated by rules we choose. It shows the product logic works. It does not show real-world lift, and the demo says so.

Use a fixed random seed so every run gives the same numbers.

## What is generated

Every number is a placeholder.

| Item | Design | Starting values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers | Seven archetypes, below | 5,000 for training, about 200 loaded into the app |
| Merchants | One lender, one insurer, plus other merchants where everyday payments happen | Northfield Finance, Harbour Life |
| Loans | One EMI per borrower, due date fixed by the lender | Rs 5,000 to Rs 40,000. Due on the 2nd, 5th, 7th, 10th or 15th |
| Policies | One monthly premium for a subset | Rs 1,000 to Rs 6,000, 15-day grace period |
| Other payments | Frequent smaller payments at other merchants, made when the balance allows | Any size |
| Rails | Loans on bank mandates, 2-day result. Premiums on UPI Autopay, instant result | Decided |
| Reason codes | Clear codes, plus catch-all codes whose true cause is kept as ground truth, each with a bank free-text line | 15% of failures carry a catch-all code |
| Non-balance failures | Cancelled or paused mandate, closed account, bank downtime | A small share of failures |

## Customer archetypes

| Archetype | Share | Pattern | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Salaried, fixed date | 45% | Salary on a set day (1st, 5th, 7th, 10th or 25th), within a day | Core flow and salary recognition |
| Salaried, last working day | 15% | Salary on the 28th to 31st | Month-end anchor |
| Gig, weekly payouts | 10% | A payout most Tuesdays | Irregular income |
| Self-employed, irregular | 10% | Credits on scattered days | Wide spread, so fail open |
| Job changer | 5% | The pay date moves once | Drift |
| Two paydays a month | 5% | The 1st and the 15th | Wide spread, so fail open |
| New or thin data | 10% | Under 3 months of history | Tier C |

## How each customer is generated

1. Create 24 months of daily balance. Income arrives by archetype, spending drains it, and the EMI or premium hits on its due date.
2. Record a payment on a day only if the balance covers it. A debit fails for insufficient funds when it does not.
3. Add noise: salary delays of 1 to 3 days, bank downtime windows, cancelled mandates, catch-all codes, refunds, tiny test amounts.
4. Keep the daily balance and each failure's true cause as ground truth. The model and the agents never see them.

## Labels and leakage

For each failed debit, the lower bound is the last failed attempt day and the upper bound is the first successful day. With no success in the cycle, the upper bound stays open (the customer is "censored": we only know the answer is later than the window). Train on these bounds. Evaluate against the true funded day.

Use only data dated before each attempt. Leakage means letting information from after the prediction date into the features, which makes a model look better than it is.

## Features

Compute the same block for four windows: last 3, 6 and 12 months, and all history.

| Feature | Captures |
| --- | --- |
| Transaction count | How much evidence exists |
| Total amount; median amount (log scale, capped at the 5th and 95th percentile); 90th-percentile amount | Size of flow |
| Share of transactions in days 1 to 7, 8 to 15, 16 to 23, 24 to month-end | Where activity clusters |
| Failure count, share of insufficient-funds failures | How often the customer runs short |
| Late-rescue count | Failures later paid: the strongest timing evidence |

Then add:

- **Comparisons across windows:** 3-month to 12-month ratios of count and amount; change in failure share; drift between the recent and long-run day pattern.
- **Day pattern:** the circular median and spread from `lib/engine/circular.ts`, start-anchored and end-anchored; the distance from the due date to the next funded day.
- **This debit:** amount and its ratio to the customer's median amount; rail; attempt number.
- **This lender's own history for the loan:** count of on-time and rescued EMIs, and the usual rescue day.
- **Salary signal:** recognized or not, and its day. Never the amount as a feature shown anywhere outside `ml/`.
- **Calendar:** month length, month-end flag.

No demographic or location feature exists anywhere.

## Training and evaluation

| Item | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Split | By time. Months 1 to 12 serve only as history. Train on failures in months 13 to 18, tune on 19 to 21, test on 22 to 24 |
| Baseline | The median-day rule: circular median and spread, the same rule as `estimateFunding` |
| Models | XGBoost AFT on the lower and upper bounds, and XGBoost quantile on the first successful day. Keep the one with the lower day error and coverage closest to 80% |
| Likelihood by day | For each of days 0 to 30, the chance the customer is funded by that day |
| Report | Day error for all three; 80% range coverage; calibration of each band; share passing the gate at each confidence floor; failed presentations per EMI collected against the original retries; how many catch-all codes the rules and lookups resolve |

The likelihood bands shown to lenders rest on calibration: a stated 80% should happen about 80% of the time. The model card must show it.

A stored prediction has the shape of `ModelScore` in `lib/engine/estimate.ts`: `h`, `u`, `likelihoodByDay` (31 numbers), `confidence`, `modelVersion`.
