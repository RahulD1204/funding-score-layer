# Model card: fsl-quantile-v1.0-s20260402

Synthetic data only. These numbers show the logic works on invented customers generated by rules we chose. They do not show real-world lift.

## What it predicts

For a failed EMI or premium debit, the chance the customer is funded by each day from the due date (day 0) to day 30. The app reads `h` (first day at 50% or more), `u` (first day at 85% or more, at least `h + 1`) and a confidence: the likelihood within 2 days of `h`.

## Data and split

- 5,000 training customers in 7 archetypes, 24 months of daily history (April 2024 to March 2026), seed 20260402.
- Train: Failed debits due April 2025 to September 2025 (months 13 to 18). Tune: October 2025 to December 2025 (months 19 to 21). Test: January 2026 to March 2026 (months 22 to 24). Months 1 to 12 are history only.
- Test rows: 5,626 failed due-date debits with an insufficient-funds code.
- Labels: the last failed attempt day and the first successful day (a retry or a payment link). The true funded day is used only to evaluate, and to calibrate on the tune months.
- 200 app customers, including the ten seeded cases, never enter training.

## Day error and range coverage (test months)

Day error is the average number of days between the predicted funded day and the true one.

| Method | Day error | Median error | Within 1 day | Day error, eligible cases | 80% range coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Median-day rule (baseline) | 3.76 | 1 | 52.7% | 1.14 | 83.7% |
| XGBoost AFT | 5.70 | 4 | 29.0% | 2.48 | 47.1% |
| XGBoost quantile | 2.60 | 1 | 51.3% | 1.21 | 72.7% |
| Chosen (quantile), calibrated | 2.51 | 1 | 54.3% | 1.12 | 77.3% |

The 80% range runs from the first day the likelihood reaches 10% to the first day it reaches 90%. Share of cases funded by the first day the likelihood reaches 80%: Median-day rule (baseline) 93.0%, XGBoost AFT 46.5%, XGBoost quantile 89.6%, Chosen (quantile), calibrated 88.1%. These sit above 80% because likelihood moves in whole-day steps, so the first day at or above 80% often holds more.

**Chosen: quantile.** Chosen on the tune months, never the test months: tune day error AFT 6.09 against quantile 2.75; tune coverage AFT 46.2% against quantile 72.7% (both measures agree).

Why AFT does badly here: the history only shows retries on days 3, 6 and 9, so most labels say "funded somewhere between day 9 and day 24". AFT fits such a range by aiming at its middle, so customers funded late in the month are predicted around day 14. The quantile model learns the first successful day directly and does not have that bias.

## Salary recognition (test months)

- Recognized: 1,229. Right day (within 1 day of the true funded day): 1,016. Recognized where no monthly salary exists: 2.
- Precision 82.7%, recall 49.1%. The rule is strict on purpose: a wrong payday is worse than none.

## Catch-all reason codes (months 13 to 24, every presentation)

- Catch-all failures: 12,694. Labelled by the code table and lookups: 786 (6.2%), of which 100.0% match the true cause.
- Left for the Triage agent: 11,908. True causes of those: balance 99.2%, instrument_closed 0.8%.
- Lookups resolve only failures with a cancelled or paused mandate or a bank downtime. Most catch-all failures are plain lack of funds, which only the agent (reading the bank's text) can label.

## Checks

- The Python median-day rule against `estimateFunding`: 500 sampled test failures and 249 app obligations, 0 mismatches.

## Limits

- Synthetic customers follow rules we wrote, so the model partly learns those rules back. Real data will be noisier.
- The labels depend on Razorpay's original retry days (3, 6, 9 and 1, 2, 3), which are placeholders to confirm.
- No demographic or location feature exists. The salary amount is not a feature: only whether a salary is recognized, and its day.
